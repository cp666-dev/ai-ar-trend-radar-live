This cluster sits right on the line between euphoric, uplifting energy and country, with both descriptors scoring almost identically (0.509 and 0.508). Underneath that, the tags for contemporary r&b, melodic rap, and sad and melancholic all score close behind, so this is not a pure country room. It reads more like emotive, songwriter-leaning material with country coloring. The mean energy of 0.46 points to restrained, mid to low intensity production rather than anthemic loudness, and the BPM range is very wide, from about 61.5 to 161.5, which tells you the grouping is driven by mood and texture more than a single tempo. Common keys lean bright with A major and E major alongside F minor and G# major. You can hear the shape in tracks like "You Only Call When It's Raining" by Lucas Ball, "Why'd You Have To Bring Me Flowers" by Madi Diaz, and "Over country" by Lizzy McAlpine, which anchor the tender, uplifting-but-wistful center of the cluster.

On movement, this one clears the bar to be discussed as a real trend. We have 8 days of history, which is above the 3 day minimum, and the measured growth is 39.6 percent with a z score of 1.94, meaning today's activity is close to two standard deviations above the recent baseline. That is a meaningful lift, not noise. Release recency supports it too, since most of the representative tracks landed in September 2026, with several from September 15 to September 25, so the cluster is being fed by fresh material rather than catalog. I would call this early and rising rather than fully proven. To harden the read, I would want the growth and elevated z score to hold or climb over the next several days rather than spike and fade.

How a call is made

charts say what won; audio says what is winning, six steps run daily, unattended python · clap · scikit-learn · sqlite · zero servers
01ingest

~1,500 track appearances from Deezer charts, editorial playlists, Apple's most-played feed and Reddit. Only releases from the last 60 days go on the radar.

02listen

Each 30-second preview becomes an audio fingerprint via CLAP, a model that hears production, instrumentation and mood. Tempo, key and energy come along for free.

03group

Fingerprints are clustered, then matched to persistent sound families so a sound keeps its identity from one day to the next.

04name

Families are named by comparing their fingerprint against hundreds of genre and mood phrases. Names describe what the model hears, not what a label claims.

05measure

Each family's share of the day's intake is tracked against its 7-day baseline. Growth ≥ 40% at z ≥ 2 is a flag. Raw counts alone never are.

06explain

Claude writes an analyst note for the top sounds from the measured statistics only, so it cannot invent a viral moment that did not happen.

Verified by ear

78% genre purity, metal 20/20

Before anything was built on the embeddings, clustering was tested by hand on 100 tracks across five distinct genres. Most remaining "errors" are the model refusing to believe Deezer's classical chart, which contains Pitbull. The model is right.

Under the hood

One cron, no servers

A GitHub Actions job runs ingestion, embedding, clustering and publishing every morning and ships this static site. Playback fetches fresh preview URLs at click time because stored ones expire within hours.

RADA/R

A solo-built, self-running product: multi-source ingestion, CLAP audio embeddings, persistent sound families, baseline-relative velocity and Claude analyst notes, rebuilt daily by an unattended pipeline.
