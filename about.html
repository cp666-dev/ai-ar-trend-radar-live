RADAR Radar Track record How it works DAILY RUN · 2026-09-14

Charts tell you what won.
Audio tells you what's winning.

Chartmetric and Soundcharts track positions — top-down, after the fact. This radar works bottom-up from the audio itself: if a production style is quietly accumulating tracks across platforms, it shows up here as a growing cluster long before any chart moves.

The pipeline, plainly

1 · Ingest~1,500 track appearances pulled daily from Deezer genre charts and editorial new-music playlists, Apple's most-played feed, and Reddit music communities. Every appearance (source, list, rank, day) is stored — that's the raw material for measuring momentum.
2 · ListenEach track's 30-second preview is embedded with CLAP (laion/clap-htsat-unfused), a model that maps audio and language into the same space — it hears production, instrumentation and mood. Three 10-second windows are averaged into one whole-clip fingerprint. Tempo, key and energy are extracted alongside.
3 · GroupEach day's tracks are clustered on those fingerprints (k swept by silhouette score). Clusters are matched to persistent sound families by centroid similarity, so "this sound" keeps its identity from day to day.
4 · NameFamilies are named by the audio itself: the cluster's fingerprint is compared against hundreds of genre and mood descriptions through CLAP's text tower. Zero LLM cost, and the names describe what the model hears.
5 · MeasureEach family's share of the daily intake is tracked against its trailing 7-day baseline. Growth ≥40% with z ≥ 2 flags a trend call — counts alone never do, because ingest volume varies day to day.
6 · ExplainClaude writes a three-sentence analyst note for the top sounds — the sound, why it's moving, what it bridges — from a prompt that contains only the measured statistics and track list, so it can't invent viral moments that didn't happen.

Trust, verified by ear

Before anything was built on top of the embeddings, the clustering was validated on 100 tracks from five deliberately distinct genres: 78% genre purity, with metal separating 20/20. The full report is listenable — judge it yourself. Notably, most "errors" are the model refusing to believe Deezer's classical chart, which contains Pitbull and Dean Martin. The model is right.

Engineering notes

Python · CLAP + librosa · scikit-learn · SQLite · Claude API · zero servers: a GitHub Actions cron runs the whole pipeline daily and publishes this static site. Deezer preview URLs expire within hours, so playback fetches fresh URLs client-side at click time. Sound families use EMA-updated centroids so names and history survive day-to-day cluster reshuffles.

Built solo as a working product: Deezer/iTunes/Reddit ingestion → CLAP audio embeddings → persistent sound families → velocity vs 7-day baseline → Claude analyst notes, regenerated daily by an unattended pipeline. Clustering validation (listenable, 78% genre purity). Previews stream fresh from their source platforms at click time and belong to their rights holders.
