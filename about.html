Chart trackers work top-down from positions, after the fact. Radar works bottom-up from the audio itself: a production style quietly accumulating tracks shows up here before any chart moves. Everything below runs unattended, every morning, on one operator's laptop budget.

Six steps, daily

the whole pipeline, in the order it runs every morning · 06:30 UTC
01ingest

~1,500 track appearances from Deezer charts, editorial playlists, Apple's most-played feed and Reddit. Everything is stored; only releases from the last 60 days go on the radar. Charts stay in as ground truth for scoring calls.

02listen

Each 30-second preview becomes an audio fingerprint via CLAP, a model that hears production, instrumentation and mood. Tempo, key and energy come along for free.

03group

Fingerprints are clustered, then matched to persistent sound families, so "this sound" keeps its identity from one day to the next.

04name

Families are named by comparing their fingerprint against hundreds of genre and mood phrases. The names describe what the model hears, not what a label wrote.

05measure

Each family's share of the day's intake is tracked against its 7-day baseline. Growth ≥ 40% at z ≥ 2 is a flag. Raw counts alone never are.

06explain

Claude writes an analyst note for the top sounds from the measured statistics only, so it cannot invent viral moments that aren't in the data.

Proof & plumbing

tested by ear before anything was built on it 100 tracks · 5 genres · blind
Verified by ear

78% genre purity, metal 20/20

Before anything was built on the embeddings, clustering was tested by hand on 100 tracks across five distinct genres. Most remaining "errors" are the model refusing to believe Deezer's classical chart, which contains Pitbull. The model is right.

Under the hood

Zero servers, zero analysts

Python · CLAP + librosa · scikit-learn · SQLite · Claude API. A GitHub Actions cron runs everything daily and publishes this static site. Playback fetches fresh preview URLs at click time because stored ones expire within hours.
